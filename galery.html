EXAMPLES OF ALTERNATIVE THERAPIES

Flower Therapy

Concept: Flower therapy uses essences prepared from flowers and is used mainly to support emotional balance and promote well-being.

Scientific evidence: Flower therapy is part of the Integrative and Complementary Practices (PICS) offered by Brazil's public health system (SUS). However, the available scientific evidence has not yet consistently shown that flower essences are effective in treating specific diseases.

Age restriction / who can receive it: There is no universal minimum age. Use by children, pregnant women and people who take medication should be assessed individually. Flower essences should not replace necessary health treatment.

Application: The essences are usually taken as drops, according to the product and the guidance received.

Who applies it: Professionals trained in the practice, following the rules of their profession. Guidance should take into account the safety of the person receiving the product.

Reflexology

Concept: Reflexology, also called reflexotherapy, uses stimulation and massage on certain areas of the body, mainly the feet and hands, to promote relaxation and well-being.

Scientific evidence: There are studies on possible benefits of reflexology, mainly related to relaxation and relief of some symptoms. However, there is not enough evidence that reflex points can treat diseases in specific organs.

Age restriction / who can receive it: It can be considered for different age groups. People with injuries, infections, changes in sensitivity or circulatory problems should be assessed beforehand.

Application: It is done through pressure and massage on specific points, mainly on the feet and hands.

Who applies it: A professional trained in reflexotherapy, working within the limits of their professional qualification.

Yoga

Concept: An ancient practice that originated in India and combines body postures, breathing exercises and meditation, seeking to integrate physical and mental aspects.

Scientific evidence: Studies indicate that yoga can contribute to well-being, stress control, relaxation, sleep and some aspects of physical health. Its benefits may vary depending on the person and the condition studied.

Age restriction / who can receive it: It can be practiced by children, teenagers, adults and older adults, with adaptations. People with physical limitations or specific health conditions should seek professional guidance before doing more demanding postures.

Application: It involves body postures, breathing exercises, relaxation and meditation, adapted to the needs of the participants.

Who applies it: Qualified yoga instructors or teachers. When offered as health care, the applicable professional qualifications and responsibilities must be respected.

Hypnotherapy

Concept: Hypnotherapy uses hypnosis as a therapeutic resource. The technique seeks to produce a state of focused attention and relaxation that can be used as part of certain health care.

Scientific evidence: Hypnosis has applications studied in some areas of health and can help in certain situations, such as pain control. Results depend on the condition treated and on how the technique is used.

Age restriction / who can receive it: The indication depends on age, purpose and the person's conditions. For children and teenagers, proper assessment and the participation of their guardians are needed, depending on the case.

Application: It uses concentration and focused-attention techniques, usually accompanied by therapeutic suggestions, according to the goal of the session.

Who applies it: A professional who is qualified and trained to use hypnosis within the limits of their profession and training.

Radiesthesia

Concept: Radiesthesia is a practice that uses objects such as pendulums and rods to try to identify supposed energies, vibrations or characteristics in people, objects and environments.

Scientific evidence: There is no robust scientific evidence that radiesthesia can detect energies or diagnose health problems. The movement of the instruments may be caused by the ideomotor effect, which involves small involuntary movements made by the person themselves.

Age restriction / who can receive it: There is no proven medical indication or established age range for treating diseases with radiesthesia. It should not be used to diagnose health problems or replace a professional assessment.

Application: Practitioners use instruments such as pendulums and rods to try to identify supposed energies or characteristics of environments and objects.

Who applies it: Radiesthesia practitioners. The practice is not equivalent to a diagnosis made by a health professional.

Biomagnetism

Concept: Biomagnetism is a practice that places magnets on certain parts of the body. Its practitioners claim that magnets can help balance the body and treat some health problems.

Scientific evidence: There is no robust scientific evidence showing that biomagnetism can balance the body's pH or eliminate microorganisms. The practice is also not among the 29 PICS currently offered by SUS.

Age restriction / who can receive it: There is no proven clinical indication for treating diseases with biomagnetism. Children, pregnant women and people with implanted medical devices should be especially careful with magnets and seek health guidance.

Application: Practitioners place magnets on certain regions of the body, claiming to seek balance in the body. The therapeutic effects claimed have not been proven.

Who applies it: Practitioners of the technique. This does not replace care from qualified health professionals.

Acupuncture

Concept: Acupuncture is a Traditional Chinese Medicine technique that uses very thin needles inserted at specific points on the body.

Scientific evidence: Acupuncture is one of the most studied integrative practices and can help mainly with some types of pain. Its effects vary depending on the condition treated. In Brazil, it is part of the PICS offered by SUS.

Age restriction / who can receive it: It can be recommended for adults, older adults and, in specific situations, children and teenagers. It requires individual assessment, especially for people with clotting disorders, who take anticoagulants or who have specific clinical conditions.

Application: Thin needles made for the technique are inserted at specific points on the body, with strict hygiene and safety care.

Who applies it: A health professional trained and qualified to perform acupuncture, according to the rules that apply to their profession.

ThetaHealing

Concept: ThetaHealing is a practice based on meditation, visualization and spiritual beliefs. Its practitioners claim the technique can help change beliefs and thoughts by accessing so-called theta brain waves.

Scientific evidence: There is not enough scientific evidence showing that ThetaHealing can "reprogram the subconscious" or produce the therapeutic effects claimed by its practitioners. The practice is not among the 29 PICS of SUS.

Age restriction / who can receive it: There is no proven clinical indication or established age range for treating diseases with ThetaHealing. Children and teenagers need appropriate protection, and the practice should not replace health follow-up.

Application: It usually involves meditation, visualization and reflection exercises related to personal and spiritual beliefs.

Who applies it: Facilitators or practitioners trained in the technique. This training alone is not equivalent to a professional health qualification.

Music Therapy

Concept: Music therapy uses music and elements such as sound, rhythm and melody in a therapeutic way. It can be used to address physical, emotional, cognitive and social needs.

Scientific evidence: Studies indicate possible benefits of music therapy in different situations, including reduced anxiety and agitation in some people with dementia, as well as support for social interaction, emotions and well-being. Results vary depending on the condition and the intervention used.

Age restriction / who can receive it: It can be used with children, teenagers, adults and older adults, according to each person's needs. The approach must be adapted to the patient's condition and ability to participate.

Application: It can involve listening to music, singing, instruments, music creation and rhythmic activities, individually or in groups.

Who applies it: A music therapist with specific training in the field. The work must respect the patient's needs and professional limits.

Phytotherapy (Herbal Medicine)

Concept: Phytotherapy uses medicinal plants and their derivatives to help prevent, treat or relieve certain health problems.

Scientific evidence: Effectiveness and safety vary depending on the plant, the product and its purpose. Some plants have studied therapeutic uses, but not all have proven effectiveness and safety. Natural products can also cause unwanted effects and interact with medications. Phytotherapy is part of the PICS offered by SUS.

Age restriction / who can receive it: The indication depends on the plant, the product and the purpose. Children, pregnant women, breastfeeding women, older adults and people who take medication need professional guidance, since there may be contraindications and interactions.

Application: It uses medicinal plants and preparations such as teas, extracts and herbal medicines, according to the product and proper guidance.

Who applies it: Guidance can be given by qualified health professionals, according to their responsibilities. Prescription and dispensing must follow the applicable rules.

Aromatherapy

Concept: Aromatherapy uses essential oils extracted from plants as a complementary resource, usually through inhalation or other forms of application, to promote well-being.

Scientific evidence: There are studies on possible effects of some essential oils in situations such as stress, anxiety, pain and sleep. However, results vary depending on the oil and the condition studied. Aromatherapy should not replace medical treatment when it is needed.

Age restriction / who can receive it: The indication depends on the essential oil, its concentration and how it is used. Children, pregnant women, breastfeeding women, and people with asthma, allergies or sensitivity to scents need special care.

Application: It can involve inhaling aromas or applying properly prepared products to the skin. Concentrated essential oils must not be swallowed or applied directly to the skin without proper guidance.

Who applies it: Qualified professionals trained in aromatherapy, following the rules of their profession and the care needed for the safe use of essential oils.
